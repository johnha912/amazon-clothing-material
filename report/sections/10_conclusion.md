# Conclusion

A product's dominant material can be recovered from its Amazon listing with useful accuracy, but it says little about how buyers will rate the product, and the rating itself says little about the material. On the ~250,000-product sample, TF-IDF with a class-weighted linear SVM reached a macro-F1 of 0.6203 and an accuracy of 0.7872 across ten materials (class-weighted logistic regression: 0.5799 and 0.7326), with leather nearly solved and the main errors among polyester, nylon, rayon, and cotton; confidence intervals are pending. For ratings, the best model, XGBoost, improved a weighted mean baseline by only 3.9% in RMSE (0.4761 against 0.4955, our arithmetic) on products with at least 5 ratings, and the random forest was worse than the baseline, because average ratings are compressed, noisy for products with few ratings, and driven by factors that listings do not show. Task C completes the picture from the buyers' side: what reviewers write about the fabric correlates only weakly with the star average (Spearman 0.292), about four in five 1-star reviews contain no material sentence the lexicon matches, and listing fields did not beat the mean baseline on RMSE (0.7% worse; MAE 0.5% better), a difference we cannot call real until the confidence intervals are in, so a star average should likely not be read as a verdict on material quality.

For anyone who wants to use this work, we recommend a TF-IDF linear model for material. The linear SVM is higher than logistic regression on this test set on both macro-F1 and accuracy, so it is the stronger choice on accuracy alone; logistic regression remains attractive for an application because its word weights can be inspected and its probabilities let it show only confident predictions, and an SVM would need calibration to do the same. The next steps are cross-validated tuning and a DistilBERT run on the same 250,000-product sample so that it can be compared fairly with the linear models. A company running this at high scale should keep the linear model as the default, since it needs no GPU and processes millions of listings cheaply, route only low-confidence listings to DistilBERT or human review, retrain whenever sellers' writing changes, and present predicted materials as suggestions rather than seller-provided facts.
