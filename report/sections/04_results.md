# Results (pilot — rerun on the 50k sample before submission)

The numbers below are pilot results on the first 10,000 metadata records (data version: `data/meta_head.jsonl pilot (10,000 rows)`, built in the shared sample schema by `analysis/load_data.py`; seed 42 throughout). They exist to validate the pipeline end to end and will be replaced by the 50,000-product sample numbers; the commands in each results file reproduce them exactly.

For Task A, the TF-IDF logistic regression reached a macro-F1 of 0.360 (accuracy 0.587) on 903 test listings across nine material classes, and the linear SVM was nearly identical at a macro-F1 of 0.357 (accuracy 0.618). Per-class scores from the logistic regression show where the signal lives: leather is easy to recognize (F1 0.844), cotton is moderately learnable (F1 0.640), and polyester, the largest class, reaches only 0.554 because generic marketing language surrounds it. The three smallest classes — polyurethane, spandex, and wool — scored zero, a direct consequence of class imbalance rather than of the representation, and the main motivation for merging rare fibers into the shared class list and for comparing against DistilBERT, whose contextual representation should separate the fiber vocabulary from the surrounding sales copy.

For Task B, the honest headline is that listing fields barely predict ratings. Predicting the weighted training mean for every product gives an RMSE of 0.586 and an MAE of 0.383 on the test split. The random forest did not beat that baseline on either metric (RMSE 0.587, MAE 0.387), and XGBoost was the only model to improve both, reaching an RMSE of 0.578 and an MAE of 0.377 — a gain of about one and a half percent. This matches the exploratory analysis: ratings are compressed near the top of the scale and are driven mostly by factors a listing cannot show, such as fit, sizing accuracy, and shipping. Material, price, and department together explain only a small part of rating variation, and the report's discussion treats this as a finding rather than a failure.

The merged comparison table for Task A will add Yanglin's DistilBERT macro-F1 from `results/material_bert.csv` once the 50,000-product sample lands; the final versions of both tables are generated by rerunning `python analysis/material_tfidf.py` and `python analysis/rating.py` on that sample.
