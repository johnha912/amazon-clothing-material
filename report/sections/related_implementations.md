# Related Implementations

Public Kaggle notebooks on Amazon Reviews 2023 mostly predict star ratings from review text, build recommenders, or describe the data; in our search of public notebooks we found none that predicts a product's material or its average rating from the listing alone, so the three below are the closest neighbors.

"Multi-Class Star Rating Prediction on Amazon Video Games Reviews using PySpark" by Keerthana Uppala (https://www.kaggle.com/code/keerthanauppala00/cn7030-multi-class-star-rating-prediction-on-amazo), copied from an earlier notebook by Wajahat Waheed, predicts the star rating of each review in a 200,000-review sample of the Video Games category with TF-IDF features and a random forest on PySpark, reaching 63.71% accuracy and 49.61% F1. The model reads the very review whose rating it predicts, so the author's opinion is already in the input, whereas our Task B uses no feature derived from the product's own reviews or ratings.

"Recommendation system Optimised" by PandeyMritunjay (https://www.kaggle.com/code/pandeymritunjay/recommendation-system-optimised) works on the Amazon Fashion category, about 826,000 products, and serves recommendations from product text built from the title, features, and description (as ours is) through a fine-tuned MPNet encoder and a FAISS index. It has no train/test split or supervised metric, and it drops the price column because about 94% of prices are missing there, whereas we keep price with a `has_price` indicator and score every model on one seeded split.

"Amazon Reviews 2023 – Analyzing Visual Keywords" by Suraj Neupane (https://www.kaggle.com/code/surajneupane/amazon-reviews-2023-analyzing-visual-keywords) explores the All Beauty reviews and finds that 29.07% of reviews of three stars or fewer mention visual keywords such as size, color, or look. It trains no model, so it does not test whether a low rating can be predicted; Task C instead measures what the reviews say about the fabric.
