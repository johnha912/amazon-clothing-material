# Discussion and limitations

Five limitations bound what these results can claim. **The label is a proxy.** "Dominant fiber" is weaker for blends where a minor fiber drives the feel, and misleading for shoes whose composition string describes the sole rather than the upper; shoes stay in scope by team decision. **Labeled listings are not a random subset.** Only 36.4% of the sample carries a composition string, and brands that publish one may write different copy, so Task A scores may not transfer to the unlabeled majority. **The cleaning step missed some composition strings.** After the stricter filter, 42 labeled listings (10 in the test set) still contain a stray percent sign in forms the labeling rule does not read, and 1,048 spell out "N percent" (Appendix B). **Average rating is a coarse target.** Task B drops the 30.7% of rated products with fewer than 5 ratings, so its results describe products with more ratings. **Material sentiment comes from a word list.** The lexicon misses words it has no entry for and counts some shoe-part sentences as fabric feel (examples in Appendix B), errors that we think mostly shrink or mis-target the sentence set, though we did not test whether they ever flip a label. We measured its agreement with another model (itself not ground truth), not its error rate, and the disagreements are mostly one-directional (16,298 lexicon-positive and SST-2-negative sentences, against 5,327 the other way).

DistilBERT used one training seed per setting (the intervals cover test sampling, not retraining noise), an epoch search that stopped at its upper bound of 3 with validation macro-F1 still rising, and cut 19.55% of test listings at 256 word pieces (`results/material_bert_run.json`).
