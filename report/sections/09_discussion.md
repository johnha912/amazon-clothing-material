# Discussion and limitations

Five limitations bound what these results can claim. **The label is a proxy.** "Dominant fiber" is clear for a 90/10 shirt, weaker for blends where a minor fiber drives the feel, and misleading for shoes whose composition string describes the sole rather than the upper. Shoes stay in scope by team decision, and the sole/upper ambiguity is flagged wherever the label is interpreted. **Labeled listings are not a random subset.** Only 36.4% of the ~250k sample (46% of the pilot) carry a composition string, and brands that publish one may write different copy, so Task A scores may not transfer to the unlabeled majority. **The shared cleaning step missed some composition strings.** `src/labels.strip_composition` left a percentage in 13,373 of 90,979 labeled listings, which inflated Task A macro-F1 by about 0.06 to 0.07 before `analysis/load_data.py` blanked every clause holding a digit followed by a percent sign, emptying the text of 405 labeled listings that we keep; 42 labeled listings (10 in the test set) still contain a stray percent sign in forms the labeling rule does not read, such as "%100 cotton" or "lOO%", and 1,048 spell out "N percent". Scores use the cleaned text; a stricter cleaner is left for later, and the shared helper in `src/` should be fixed so every script is clean by default. **Average rating is a coarse target.** It mixes product quality with sizing, expectations, delivery, and the noise of small rating counts; Task B drops products with fewer than 5 ratings to reduce that noise, which removes 30.7% of rated products, so its results describe products with more ratings and are not comparable with the earlier unfiltered run. The modest Task B result shows that listings carry little rating signal, not that buyers ignore material, and Task C measures the material part directly. **Material sentiment comes from a word list.** The lexicon misses words it has no entry for ("the material is so irritating" scores neutral and is dropped) and counts some shoe-part sentences as fabric feel ("the sole is soft"), errors that we think mostly shrink or mis-target the sentence set, though we did not test whether they ever flip a label. The share of positive material sentences rises monotonically with the review's own stars (17.8% at 1 star to 94.8% at 5), which is consistent with the polarity it does assign, and we measured its agreement with another model, not its error rate: a pretrained sentiment model (DistilBERT fine-tuned on SST-2, itself not ground truth) run on the same 209,830 sentences agrees with the lexicon on 85.3% of the 146,981 sentences where the lexicon takes a side (Cohen's kappa 0.617). The disagreements are mostly one-directional: 16,298 sentences are lexicon-positive and SST-2-negative, against 5,327 the other way.

The DistilBERT result also has limits of its own: one training seed per setting (the intervals cover test-listing sampling, not retraining noise), an epoch search that stopped at its upper bound of 3 with validation macro-F1 still rising, and 19.55% of test listings longer than 256 word pieces and cut (`results/material_bert_run.json`). The Task A model is best used as a search and accessibility aid: it recovers a field sellers already state in prose. Its predictions should be shown with their confidence and never stored as seller-provided facts, especially for regulated claims such as leather or wool content.
