# Data analysis

We use the product metadata of the Clothing, Shoes and Jewelry category of Amazon Reviews 2023 (Hou et al.). All numbers in this section come from `data/sample.parquet` (249,762 rows, `make_sample.py` 2219c1e, seed 42), computed with `python analysis/eda.py` and short pandas commands on the output of `analysis/load_data.py` (our arithmetic); the pipeline figure and class counts are in Appendix B.

A listing's label is the known fiber with the highest stated percentage (ties go to the fiber listed first); listings without a composition string get no label and are left out of Task A rather than guessed, and 90,979 of 249,762 (36.4%) receive one. `src/labels.py` merges the fibers into ten classes: cotton (33.0% of labeled listings), polyester (31.3%), leather (17.3%), nylon (5.9%), rayon (5.3%), other (3.8%), spandex (2.1%), wool (0.8%), rubber (0.3%), and metal (0.2%).

Model text joins the title, bullets, and description with every clause containing a percentage removed. The shared `strip_composition` in `src/labels.py` removes only strings of the form "90% Polyester", so it left a percentage sign in 13,373 of the 90,979 labeled listings (14.7%), and those leftovers leak the label: a first run on insufficiently cleaned text scored macro-F1 0.6397 (logistic regression) and 0.6932 (SVM), about 0.06 and 0.07 above the cleaned scores (our arithmetic from the two runs). `analysis/load_data.py` therefore blanks every clause that holds a digit followed by a percent sign (its self-check passes), leaving 405 labeled listings with empty text, which we keep.

![Mean rating by dominant material (materials with at least 30 listings), from `python analysis/eda.py` on the 249,762-row sample.](../../results/eda/02_rating_by_material.png){width=33%}

Ratings are compressed near the top: the mean is 4.08, the standard deviation 0.75, and the mean rating by material spans only 4.056 (spandex) to 4.226 (wool). An average of $n_i$ ratings has variance $\sigma^2 / n_i$, and products with at most 5 ratings (34.6% of the sample) have a rating standard deviation of 1.10, against 0.34 for the 26.7% with at least 50, so Task B keeps only products with at least 5 ratings and weights each training product by $s_i = \log(1 + n_i)$. Prices are missing for 79.8% of listings, not at random (unpriced products average 4.06 stars against 4.17 for priced ones), so the Task B features are log price, a has-price flag, title and text word counts, department, and the material label ("unknown" if unlabeled).
